\title{Elliptic-shift hyperboloidal evolution in spherical symmetry:
formulation, numerical verification, and strong-field limitations}
\date{Working manuscript; evidence snapshot of 26 October 2026}
\begin{abstract}
We investigate a spherical free-evolution formulation of the conformally compactified Einstein equations in which a live hyperboloidal lapse is coupled to an elliptic shift derived from the conformal metric connection. The shift condition does not constrain the independently evolved Z4 connection variable. We describe nonlinear spherical equations, actual-null-infinity boundary treatment, positive field variables, a separate asymptotically flat puncture chart, and coupled massless-scalar evolution. Independent tensor comparisons, stage-by-stage shift solves, and restart tests support implementation consistency. A finite-amplitude Minkowski lapse pulse exhibits observed orders of $5.00$ in the bulk and $4.20$ near null infinity over $0\leq t\leq1$, although the finest constraint measurements encounter an arithmetic floor. A coupled subcritical scalar experiment gives a final mass--flux discrepancy of $8.19\times10^{-9}$ on the finest tested grid. Historical wormhole trials develop unresolved steep gauge fronts, and causal retained-domain evolutions have insufficient constraint convergence despite reaching $t=10$. A subsequent spherical conformal telegrapher lapse adds a relaxation flux and tracks the coupled gauge characteristics at every Runge--Kutta stage. We report a full-domain, three-resolution follow-up through $t=10$, with bulk field order $5.89$ and finest peak accepted Hamiltonian RMS $1.53\times10^{-8}$. The fixed candidate passes the stated spatial checks, while retaining a narrow nonmonotone inner lapse structure and strong finite-grid characteristic compression. A single-grid continuation targeting $t=500$ is still running at this snapshot. An apparent-horizon-exterior budget remains nearly steady from $t=10$ to $t=20$, with final relative horizon-mass error $6.43\times10^{-8}$; large interior errors are recorded separately and excluded from that budget. Independent quadrature, dissipation-amplitude, and adjacent relaxation-parameter controls remain unrun; robust nonlinear stability is therefore not established. An already-trumpet control with a separate analytic lapse source reaches $t=100$ with small invariant errors, but does not establish wormhole-to-trumpet evolution in the main gauge. We retain negative results and distinguish algebraic verification, short-time convergence, and unresolved nonlinear stability. No successful coupled collapse, quasinormal-mode extraction, or late-time-tail result is claimed.
\end{abstract}
