MeshBlock cures the constraint-invalid Brill-collapse Figure-3 result.  N128,

\section{Interpretation and next step}

The identical interface geometry becomes markedly better behaved as cells per
MeshBlock increase.  N512 moves the peak toward the published amplitude,
reduces peak constraints by about an order of magnitude relative to N256,
suppresses rho near 5 numerical content, and survives through the full
Figure-3 interval.  Bulk/parent under-resolution is therefore a major driver
of the N256 failure and is not ruled out.

This does not prove that AMR interfaces or the axis are harmless.  N512 still
has an order-unity constraint episode at the peak, and the global maxima are
axis-adjacent.  A residual interface or axis contribution can coexist with
bulk under-resolution.

The natural next step is a bounded shared-RHS audit at the retained N512 peak
state, separating active-axis stencils, same-level seams, and coarse-fine ghost
and interface contributions.  Gauge, KO, transfer, positivity checks, and the
exact tree should remain unchanged.
