\begin{abstract}
The \texttt{spherical\_cmc} physics module implements a vacuum, spherically
symmetric constrained evolution on compactified constant-mean-curvature slices.
The Hamiltonian and momentum constraints and CMC lapse are solved through their
radial first integrals; a differential elliptic shift controls the metric-derived
Dirac connection. Exact boundary values keep future null infinity fixed and null.
The module uses AthenaK's mesh, Runge--Kutta coefficients, task graph, outputs,
and restart infrastructure, and reuses the fork's hyperboloidal tensor kernels.
Its present scope is one radial block, double precision, and host computation.
It is a numerical research prototype, not a freely evolving Z4c system or a proof
of the earlier proposed three-dimensional hyperboloidal initial-boundary problem.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope and relation to the existing fork}
The implementation branches from \texttt{z4c\_hyperboloidal}, revision
\texttt{ca77b353a60a} (25 October checkout). It adds an exclusive physics
module, selected by an input block \texttt{<spherical\_cmc>}, without changing
the existing Cauchy Z4c or hyperboloidal evolution choices. The new native module
is distinct from the pre-existing standalone spherical driver.

Spherical vacuum gravity has no radiative degrees of freedom. Here the mass
and CMC momentum integration constant are fixed, while a nonstationary radial
coordinate distortion evolves under an elliptic gauge. The two supported
families are regular-center Minkowski geometry and excised Schwarzschild CMC
trumpets. This is a useful test of scri limits, constrained stage projection,
elliptic gauge evolution, and black-hole excision; it is not a gravitational-wave
or matter-collapse test. A lapse pulse independent of the constraints is not
an allowed datum in this formulation.

The design follows the CMC/conformal-Dirac direction of the earlier report.
Published constrained hyperboloidal formulations provide motivation
\cite{moncrief,rinne}, while CMC/spatial-harmonic well-posedness on other domains
\cite{andersson} cannot be transferred without a new scri boundary argument.

\section{Notation and the change from ordinary Z4c}
Following the physical and spatial-conformal notation of Daszuta et al.,
Sec.~3 \cite{daszuta}, use signature $(-,+,+,+)$,
$K_{ij}=-\frac12\mathcal L_n\gamma_{ij}$, and
\begin{align}
 \chi&=\psi^{-4}, & \tg_{ij}&=\chi\gamma_{ij}, &
 \tilde A_{ij}&=\chi(K_{ij}-\tfrac13 K\gamma_{ij}), &
 \widehat K&=K-2\Theta.
\end{align}
Bars denote a separate Penrose compactification, not the spatial conformal
transformation:
\begin{equation}
 \bar g_{ab}=\Om^2g_{ab},\quad \ba=\Om\alpha,\quad
 \bc=\Om^{-2}\chi,\quad \Ac_{ij}=\Om^{-1}\tilde A_{ij}.
\end{equation}
The module fixes $\Om=(1-r^2)/2$, $0\leq r\leq1$, $K=-3$, and units in which
the scri radius and CMC curvature scale equal one. The shift is unchanged by
Penrose rescaling. In Cartesian tensor components, with $n_i=x_i/r$,
\begin{equation}
 \tg_{ij}=b\delta_{ij}+(a-b)n_i n_j,\qquad b=a^{-1/2},\qquad \det\tg=ab^2=1.
 \label{eq:metric}
\end{equation}
Thus the spherical line element is
$\bar\gamma_{ij}dx^idx^j=\bc^{-1}(a\,dr^2+b r^2d\omega_2^2)$.
The code variable \texttt{scmc\_a} is $a$; it is not a curvature length scale.

The changes from free Z4c evolution are explicit:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{p{0.27\linewidth}p{0.65\linewidth}}
\toprule
Ordinary Z4c quantity & Treatment in this module\\\midrule
$\tg_{ij}$ & Only its spherical radial eigenvalue $a$ is evolved; determinant one is algebraic.\\
$\chi,\tilde A_{ij}$ & Replace their evolution by radial vacuum constraint reconstruction of $\bc,\Ac_{ij}$.\\
$\widehat K,\Theta$ & Set $\widehat K=-3$, $\Theta=0$ at every stage.\\
Evolved connection & Replace by the metric-derived contracted conformal connection; $Z_i=0$ is imposed.\\
Lapse driver & Use the CMC lapse solution, with $\ba|_{\scri}=1$.\\
Gamma driver & Replace by the elliptic Dirac-relaxation shift below.\\
Tangential shift & Zero by spherical symmetry.\\\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The projected variables are stored for native output and restart, but are not
independent evolved degrees of freedom. Existing full tensor equations are used
as independent differential compatibility diagnostics. This distinction is
essential when interpreting constraint propagation and hyperbolicity.

\section{Solving the constraints and the CMC lapse}
Let $R$ be the physical areal radius, $U=1/R$, and
\begin{equation}
 D(R)=1-\frac{2M}{R}+\left(-R+\frac{C}{R^2}\right)^2,
 \qquad F(U)=U^2D(1/U)=U^2-2MU^3+(-1+CU^3)^2.
\end{equation}
In areal coordinates the physical spatial metric is
$dR^2/D+R^2d\omega_2^2$. The trace-free physical extrinsic curvature has mixed
eigenvalues $(-2C/R^3,C/R^3,C/R^3)$. Hence
\begin{align}
 {}^{(3)}R&=\frac{2(1-D-RD')}{R^2}=-6+\frac{6C^2}{R^6},\\
 A_{ij}A^{ij}&=\frac{6C^2}{R^6},\qquad
 {}^{(3)}R-A_{ij}A^{ij}+\tfrac23K^2=0,\\
 \partial_R(-2C/R^3)+\frac2R(-2C/R^3-C/R^3)&=0.
\end{align}
The last equation is the radial momentum constraint.

Matching the angular and radial metric components gives
\begin{equation}
 \frac{dR}{dr}=\frac{a^{3/4}R\sqrt D}{r},\qquad
 \tau(r)=\int_r^1\frac{a(s)^{3/4}}s\,ds=-\log r+J(r),\qquad
 J(r)=\int_r^1\frac{a(s)^{3/4}-1}s\,ds.
\end{equation}
Consequently $dU/d\tau=\sqrt{F(U)}$, $U(\tau=0)=0$ is a regular scri initial
value. The reconstructed fields, for $r<1$, are
\begin{align}
 \bc&=b\left(\frac{rU}{\Om}\right)^2,&
 \Ac_{rr}&=-\frac{2aCU^3}{\Om},\label{eq:project}\\
 c_t&=a(1)^{3/4},&
 \ba&=c_t\Om\frac{\sqrt F}{U},&
 \beta_{\rm stat}^r&=c_t r a^{-3/4}(-1+CU^3).\label{eq:static}
\end{align}
The transverse eigenvalue of $\Ac_{ij}$ is $-b\Ac_{rr}/(2a)$, so its conformal
trace vanishes identically. The radial component is a Cartesian radial
eigenvalue, as used by the existing spherical tensor kernel.

The physical CMC lapse equation is
\begin{equation}
 -\Delta_\gamma\alpha+(3+6C^2/R^6)\alpha=0.
 \label{eq:lapse}
\end{equation}
Its selected solution is $\alpha=c_t\sqrt D$. Indeed
$\Delta_\gamma\sqrt D/\sqrt D=D''/2+D'/R=3+6C^2/R^6$.
An excision boundary would allow other homogeneous lapse choices; this module
selects the lapse inherited from the fixed trumpet, rather than claiming a
uniqueness theorem from the scri value alone. No lapse matrix solve is necessary
for this restricted vacuum family.

\subsection{Regular-center family}
Smoothness requires $a(0)=1$ and $a-1=O(r^2)$. Regularity selects $M=C=0$ and
$U=\sinh\tau$. The expression evaluated in the code is
\begin{equation}
 \bc=b\left[e^{-J}\left(1+\frac{\operatorname{expm1}(2J)}{1-r^2}\right)\right]^2,
 \quad \ba=c_t\sqrt{\Om^2+r^2b/\bc},\quad
 \beta_{\rm stat}^r=-r\left[\frac{a(1)}{a(r)}\right]^{3/4}.
\end{equation}
This avoids forming $U=\infty$ at the origin. The reference $a=1$ has
$\bc=1$, $\ba=(1+r^2)/2$, $\beta^r=-r$, and vanishing trace-free curvature.
The lapse calculation uses a rationalized difference from this reference to
preserve the exact stationary state to roundoff.

\subsection{Excised black-hole family}
For $M>0$ use $r_{\rm in}>0$ and the critical $C$ supplied by the fork's existing
\pathfile{CMCTrumpet}. It solves for a double zero of $D$ at the trumpet radius
and evaluates $\sqrt F$ in factored form, avoiding cancellation near that zero.
The new public method \pathfile{InverseArealDerivative} exposes that existing
first integral for reuse. Radial integration uses RK4 with conformal-distance
substeps at most $0.002$. The inner boundary must satisfy $R_{\rm in}<2M$ and
have purely inward physical characteristic flow. The example uses $M=0.5$,
$r_{\rm in}=0.05$. Neither a puncture floor nor evaluation at $r=0$ is used for
black holes.

\section{Elliptic Dirac gauge and metric evolution}
Write $u=1/a$ and let $V$ be the radial coefficient of the metric-derived
contracted conformal connection:
\begin{equation}
 \widehat\Gamma^i=Vn^i,\qquad
 V=-u'-\frac{2(u-u^{-1/2})}{r}.
 \label{eq:dirac}
\end{equation}
This connection is not a separately evolved Z4 variable. The gauge prescribes
\begin{equation}
 \partial_t V=-\eta V,\quad \eta\geq0,\qquad
 \beta^r=\beta_{\rm stat}^r+r y.
\end{equation}
The static shift and reconstructed extrinsic curvature cancel exactly in the
continuum metric equation. Its remaining part is
\begin{equation}
 \partial_t a=r\left(ya'+\tfrac43 ay'\right),\qquad
 W:=\partial_tu=r y u'-\tfrac43ur y'.
 \label{eq:evolution}
\end{equation}
Taking the variation of \eqref{eq:dirac} gives $V_t=-W'-cW$, where
$c=(2+u^{-3/2})/r$. Direct substitution yields the scalar elliptic equation
\begin{align}
 y''+P y'+Qy&=-\frac{3\eta V}{4ur},\label{eq:elliptic}\\
 P&=\frac{u'}{4u}+\frac1r+c,\qquad
 Q=-\frac3{4u}\left[u''+u'\left(\frac1r+c\right)\right].
\end{align}
The coefficient of $y''$ is one, independent of the metric and of $\Om$.
Boundary conditions are
\begin{equation}
 y(1)=0,\qquad
 \begin{cases} y'(0)=0, & M=0,\\ y(r_{\rm in})=0, & M>0.\end{cases}
 \label{eq:bc}
\end{equation}
At a regular center $P=4/r+O(r)$ and $Q$ and $V/r$ are finite. The leading
operator is the radial Laplacian in five dimensions. Even reflection supplies
the regular branch without evaluating $1/r$ at $r=0$.

Using $y$ is important numerically: evaluating $v'-v/r$ for $v=ry$ using unrelated
finite differences leaves a truncation error at the first cell that need not
respect $a_t=O(r^2)$. The form \eqref{eq:evolution} preserves the required radial
scaling. This corrected a nonconvergent center error found during development.

There is a useful continuum uniqueness argument beyond the flat reference.
For the homogeneous shift problem $V_t=0$, factorization gives
\begin{equation}
 W'+cW=0,\qquad (u^{-3/4}y)'=-\frac{3}{4r}u^{-7/4}W.
 \label{eq:factor}
\end{equation}
On the ball, $c=3/r+O(r)$, so a nonzero homogeneous $W$ behaves as $r^{-3}$.
Regularity excludes it. Then $y$ is proportional to $u^{3/4}$, and $y(1)=0$
sets that constant to zero. On an excised annulus write
$W=A\exp(-\int c\,dr)$. Integrating the second equation between two zero
Dirichlet values gives $A$ times a strictly positive integral equal to zero.
Thus $A=0$ and again $y=0$. This proves absence of a homogeneous kernel for
smooth positive $u$ satisfying the stated center regularity or annular boundary
conditions. Standard elliptic Fredholm theory then gives invertibility: on the
ball the operator is understood as a smooth radial five-dimensional elliptic
operator, since $P-4/r=O(r)$ and $Q$ is regular. Bounds require positive lower
bounds on $u$, controlled smooth coefficients, and a fixed annular inner radius;
no uniform estimate as those assumptions degenerate is asserted.

The same identities explain the reduced continuum dynamics. $V(t,r)$ is known
as $e^{-\eta t}V(0,r)$; recovering $u$ solves the first-order equation
\eqref{eq:dirac}. On the ball its regular branch fixes the integration constant.
On an annulus the conserved conformal radial distance
$I=\int_{r_{\rm in}}^1u^{-3/4}dr/r$ fixes it, since
$I_t=[u^{-3/4}y]_{r_{\rm in}}^1=0$. The derivative of $I$ with respect to the
inner value of $u$ is strictly negative: the corresponding first-order variation
is $z'= -cz$, with $z(r_{\rm in})=1$, so
$I'=-\frac34\int u^{-7/4}z\,dr/r<0$.
This gives a constructive local smooth solution on a positive branch via the
implicit function theorem, rather than a hyperbolic characteristic argument.
It is specific to the fixed-mass spherical constraint manifold. The code checks
pivots, finite values and the original matrix residual as additional discrete
safeguards; continuum invertibility alone does not validate a discretization.

Linearizing on the regular-center reference, $a=1+\epsilon f$ gives
$V=\epsilon(f'+3f/r)+O(\epsilon^2)$. Thus
$(\partial_r+3/r)(f_t+\eta f)=0$ and regularity implies $f_t=-\eta f$.
This proves bounded damping of this reduced linear gauge degree of freedom.
It does not analyze the full off-constraint Z4c symbol. On an excised annulus
there is an additional homogeneous radial distortion, and the two shift boundary
conditions determine its evolution; simple pointwise damping of $a-1$ is not
expected there.

\section{Scri fixing, regularity, and boundary interpretation}
At $r=1$ the analytic limits are imposed as exact assignments:
\begin{equation}
 \bc=a(1),\qquad \ba=1,\qquad \beta^r=-1,\qquad \Ac_{rr}=0.
 \label{eq:scri}
\end{equation}
Since $U=a(1)^{3/4}(1-r)+O((1-r)^2)$ and
$\Om=(1-r)+O((1-r)^2)$, these are the limits of
\eqref{eq:project}--\eqref{eq:static}, not floors or extrapolated values.
With $\partial_t\Om=0$,
\begin{equation}
 \bar g^{ab}\partial_a\Om\partial_b\Om
 =\Om'^2\left(\frac{\bc}{a}-\frac{(\beta^r)^2}{\ba^2}\right)=0
 \quad\hbox{at }r=1.
\end{equation}
Physical radial null coordinate speeds there are
$-\beta^r\pm\ba\sqrt{\bc/a}=0,2$: no physical characteristic enters from beyond
scri. At the excision boundary the code checks
$-\beta^r+\ba\sqrt{\bc/a}<0$ after every solve.

The gauge is elliptic, so finite-speed hyperbolic gauge characteristics are not
its appropriate boundary description. Its boundary data are explicitly fixed
by \eqref{eq:bc} and the lapse choice. Constraints are projected rather than
freely propagated; truncation error remains and is measured independently.
There are no independent incoming constraint characteristics in this reduced
state vector, but this does not prove immunity to numerical constraint errors.

Smooth reconstructed spherical data have finite scri limits. The existing
unregularized tensor diagnostic kernel is only evaluated at interior cell
centers with $\Om>0$; it is never called at the exact boundary. Its cancellations
can amplify differentiation error by $1/\Om$. In particular, a low-order
quadrature for $J$, followed by division by $\Om$ and two derivatives, generated
boundary defects during development. The present higher-order quadrature and
closures address the tested cases; they do not constitute a general uniform
error estimate for arbitrary perturbed data. A naive mass diagnostic close to
scri also suffers cancellation, so the history maximum is restricted to
$r\leq0.8$, with the unrestricted field still available for inspection.

\section{Discretization and native AthenaK integration}
There are $N$ ordinary AthenaK radial cell centers and two exact endpoint nodes:
$r_i=r_{\rm in}+(i-\frac12)h$ for $1\leq i\leq N$, with
$h=(1-r_{\rm in})/N$. The nearest ghost slots store the two endpoint metric
degrees of freedom for restart; they are not ordinary field samples at ghost
cell coordinates. Other ghost slots are zero. Field output with ghost zones is
therefore rejected.

Differential operators have fourth-order spatial accuracy on smooth data:
five-point centered formulas in the interior, six-point polynomial closures on
the unequal endpoint intervals, and reflected even/odd stencils at a regular
center. The PDE remains second order in space. Quintic interpolation and
three-point Gaussian quadrature integrate $J$ on each interval. The elliptic
matrix has fixed bandwidth; banded elimination uses $O(N)$ work and storage,
with no pivoting and explicit small-pivot rejection. The residual is measured
against the original, uneliminated operator and must be below $10^{-8}$ after
normalization by $1+|\mathrm{source}|$. A three-dimensional multigrid hierarchy
would add unnecessary machinery to this single radial solve.

The native RK3 task sequence is
\begin{center}
\texttt{CopyState $\to$ SolveGauge $\to$ RKUpdate $\to$ SolveGauge $\to$ NewTimeStep}.
\end{center}
At each stage, projection supplies the fields on which the RHS is evaluated;
after the metric update it is repeated. The endpoint $a(1)$ evolves too; fixing
it independently would change the elliptic boundary problem. The regular-center
endpoint is reset to $a(0)=1$. The task uses AthenaK's $\gamma_0,\gamma_1,\beta$
RK coefficients and the mesh time step. The reported time-step estimate is
\begin{equation}
 \Delta t_{\rm module}=\min\left\{\frac{h}{\max(1,|\beta^r|+\ba\sqrt{\bc/a})},
                         \frac1{1+\eta}\right\},
\end{equation}
with the native CFL multiplier applied by the mesh. This is an operational
conservative choice, not a stability theorem for all nonlinear data.

All radial solves currently execute on the host. Native Kokkos arrays and explicit
host/device copies keep the storage interface consistent with other modules.
GPU performance, multi-block decomposition, MPI with more than one rank, AMR,
zooming, matter coupling, and arbitrary $K$ or scri radius are not supported.
Unsupported physics combinations and integrators fail explicitly.

\section{Inputs, outputs, and reproduction}
From the repository root, a serial build and example runs are:
\begin{lstlisting}
cmake -S . -B build-cmc -DCMAKE_BUILD_TYPE=Release \
  -DAthena_ENABLE_MPI=OFF -DKokkos_ENABLE_SERIAL=ON \
  -DAthena_ENABLE_SPHERICAL_CMC_TESTS=ON
cmake --build build-cmc -j4
mkdir -p run-cmc
cd run-cmc
../build-cmc/src/athena -i ../inputs/spherical_cmc/minkowski.athinput
# Or use ../inputs/spherical_cmc/schwarzschild.athinput
\end{lstlisting}
Both examples use $a(0,r)=\exp[A r^2(1-r^2)^2]$, with $A=0.05$, $\eta=1$.
Set \pathfile{spherical_cmc/metric_pulse=0} for a stationary reference,
or \texttt{dirac\_damping=0} for a stationary distorted coordinate metric.
Keep \texttt{mesh/nx1} and \texttt{meshblock/nx1} equal when changing resolution.
The minimum radial resolution is eight cells and at least two ghost slots are
required. The mesh ends exactly at $r=1$.

Native \texttt{variable=spherical\_cmc} exposes 13 fields:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{p{0.46\linewidth}p{0.47\linewidth}}
\toprule Name & Meaning\\\midrule
\texttt{scmc\_a, scmc\_chi, scmc\_alpha, scmc\_beta} & $a,\bc,\ba,\beta^r$\\
\texttt{scmc\_Khat, scmc\_Theta, scmc\_Arr} & $-3,0,\Ac_{rr}$\\
\texttt{scmc\_Gamma} & Metric-derived $V$\\
\texttt{scmc\_H, scmc\_Mr} & Independent physical Hamiltonian and radial momentum residuals\\
\texttt{scmc\_Krhs, scmc\_Arhs} & Full tensor RHS for $K$ and $\Ac_{rr}$\\
\texttt{scmc\_mass\_error} & Spherical physical mass diagnostic minus prescribed $M$\\\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The $\Ac_{rr}$ RHS need not vanish during a black-hole gauge evolution: it must
agree with the tangent of the reconstructed $\Ac_{rr}$, which a separate test
checks. $K_t$ should vanish in the continuum. Exact endpoint diagnostics are in
the history file, not in cell-centered tables. Restart retains the endpoint
metric values and the ordinary AthenaK time-step state. Dependent fields are
recomputed after reading. \texttt{state\_version=1} identifies the payload layout;
changing the physics family or mass during restart is not a supported physical
continuation.

\section{Verification and remaining limits}
The reproducible checks are
\begin{lstlisting}
python3 tst/spherical_cmc/check_equations.py
ctest --test-dir build-cmc -R spherical_cmc --output-on-failure
ATHENA_CMC_EXE="$PWD/build-cmc/src/athena" python3 -m pytest -q \
  tst/test_suite/spherical_cmc/test_native_cpu.py
\end{lstlisting}
The symbolic script verifies the Dirac operator, the physical Hamiltonian and
momentum first integrals, the CMC lapse, exact nullness, and the regular-center
linear gauge damping identity. The C++ test checks reference preservation,
spatial refinement, rejection of nonfinite metrics, and agreement between the
constraint-projection tangent and the existing tensor $\Ac_{rr}$ equation.
The native tests cover finite-amplitude gauge evolution, exact scri nullness,
regular-center and black-hole refinement, bitwise restart equivalence (including
both native restart file organizations), and invalid configurations.

Representative double-precision runs with $A=0.05$, $\eta=1$, $t=2$ gave:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrrr}
\toprule Family & $N$ & $\|H\|_\infty$ & $\|K_t\|_\infty$\\\midrule
Regular center &32&$2.03\,10^{-7}$&$9.61\,10^{-7}$\\
 &64&$1.31\,10^{-8}$&$1.51\,10^{-7}$\\
 &128&$9.93\,10^{-10}$&$2.07\,10^{-8}$\\
Black hole, $M=0.5$ &64&$1.01\,10^{-3}$&$7.48\,10^{-6}$\\
 &128&$1.85\,10^{-4}$&$8.67\,10^{-7}$\\
 &256&$3.77\,10^{-5}$&$9.29\,10^{-8}$\\\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
These are measured errors, not a claim of fourth-order global evolution or
uniform convergence in every diagnostic. The black-hole Hamiltonian converges
more slowly than the smooth static projection; the regular-center Hamiltonian
encounters a floating-point differentiation floor at higher resolution. The
null residual at the exact outer boundary is exactly zero in these runs. The
native solver uses third-order time integration.

The global source modifications were also checked with the existing Z4c
CPU overhaul, binary-conversion and restart regressions. The radial kernel is
suitable for address/undefined-behavior sanitizer checks; hardware backend and
MPI validation remain future work. Detailed recorded commands and pass counts
are in the companion \pathfile{spherical_cmc.md}.

This module is ready for controlled spherical numerical experiments within the
stated scope. It does not establish uniform strong hyperbolicity of an
unconstrained three-dimensional Z4c system, nor nonlinear well-posedness of the
full conformal elliptic-hyperbolic scri problem. The next scientific extensions
are a consistent spherical matter sector (introducing physical dynamics),
regular mass extraction at scri, and boundary estimates for the intended
three-dimensional formulation. These must precede interpreting spherical
success as resolution of the original oblique-direction hyperbolicity problem.
