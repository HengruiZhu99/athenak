# Separate KO closure repair

The baseline uses complete fourth-difference lines, truncated at patch boundaries, in negative weighted-transpose form. A quartic gives a third-order edge RHS. The separate `HYPERBOLIC_CONSISTENT_KO=1` control uses fifth differences (KO10), preserving the same weights/taper and negative weighted-transpose form. For any smooth mapped field with bounded fifth derivatives and regular map, each complete fifth difference is O(h^5), its physical-volume line weight is O(h^3), inverse point volume is O(h^-3), and the prefactor is O(h^-1). A truncated sum therefore remains O(h^4) even at a corner. This argument applies to this measured repair after confirming the original lower-order defect; it is not an assumption that any 1/h factor causes order loss. Nonpositive discrete energy follows directly from minus B5-transpose W B5 with nonnegative W. It does not prove stability of the full hyperboloidal PDE/interface system.

Full actual composite grids at N12/18/24/32/48/64 include every block edge, evolved overlap and the continuous core. Their logical quartic tests annihilate to roundoff with KO10, the random-data weighted energy is negative, and the core smooth-field order approaches4. Outer-shell maxima approach more slowly (3.32 through3.69); coefficients vary rapidly on these coarse cells. Original KO8 full-grid results are preserved, and CPU/device parity is in `device-parity.log`.

`test_ko_shell_refinement.cpp` evaluates the same dropped-line operator on bounded finer samples of all six mapped faces of both non-overlap shells. Samples include six layers next to every edge/corner and face-interior controls, with actual Jacobian/volume weights and the unchanged taper. It does not allocate a fine full domain. The analytic smooth field is evaluated with long-double expressions (which may use double precision on some platforms); the test does not assume extra precision. At the shared N24/32/48/64 resolutions these bounded maxima reproduce the full-domain outer-shell maxima within2.3e-16 relative, including their edge neighborhood. Bounded refinement to N256 gives orders3.77,3.84,3.88,3.915 on the final four pairs; N^4 times the maximum tends toward a finite coefficient. This supports the expected fourth-order edge consistency and distinguishes slow asymptotics from the old third-order defect. It is not a claim that all finite-grid orders exceed4 or that bounded samples exhaust a full N256 mesh.

The taper is zero at r>=.8. Difference lines may straddle the taper, so points just beyond .8 can still receive a contribution from a line with nonzero weight; lines wholly beyond it vanish. No conclusion identifies this KO defect as the cause of late .775–.9 constraint error.
