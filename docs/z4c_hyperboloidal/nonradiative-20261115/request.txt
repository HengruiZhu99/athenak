Important distinction
An arbitrary coordinate transformation of Schwarzschild need not satisfy A’s
gauge equations. Therefore organize the work into two clearly separated layers:

I. Analytic-data tests of our reconstruction and extraction.
II. Native evolutions using the unchanged A equations.

A successful analytic replay does not validate A’s evolution. Conversely,
failure of an arbitrary prescribed coordinate metric to satisfy A’s gauge
equations does not show that A is incorrect.

1. Inventory and establish controls.

Reuse the existing scri recorder, geometry/frame tests, stationary-reference
tests and scri investigation diagnostics. Inspect their actual coverage.
Do not count point-kernel checks as full evolution tests.

Establish Minkowski and Schwarzschild zero-news controls. Use our actual
compactification, field conventions, surface topology and mass normalization.

For stationary evolution, check whether the chosen reference is stationary
under the configured A equations. If its coordinates relax, distinguish
coordinate relaxation from physical radiation and from discretization error.

2. Reproduce ALL THREE nonradiative Moxon test families analytically.

Implement:
 a. Rotating Schwarzschild.
 b. Bouncing Schwarzschild.
 c. Schwarzschild gauge wave.

Include untransformed Schwarzschild as their common control.

Use the paper’s transformations, parameters and expected zero-news result.
Begin with one representative case per family, then cover the reported
parameter ranges where feasible. Document any reduction of the sweep.

In particular, verify from the source:
- The rotation convention and angular velocity.
- The bouncing translation, its amplitude and period.
- The gauge-wave coordinate transformation and sine-Gaussian profile.

Convert the metrics consistently to a hyperboloidal conformal representation
with scri at our endpoint. Derive regular endpoint limits and spatial/time
derivatives analytically or with independent automatic differentiation.
Avoid numerical evaluation of divergent physical expressions directly at scri.

Provide an explicit map between the paper’s units/conventions and ours.

Test the analytic data in two ways:
- Feed exact metric derivatives into the existing geometry/frame extractor.
- Sample through the native surface/donor layout and recorder reconstruction,
  including angular seams and radial derivative support.

These distinguish formula/frame errors from sampling and derivative errors.
Use an independent analytic derivative oracle rather than validating a
routine solely against itself.

Measure news in ALL retained modes and a nodal/angular norm, not only N20.
Inspect the separate Ricci and conformal-frame contributions on identical
cuts and dyads. Large contributions are acceptable if their physical sum
converges to zero.

If an analytic initial cut is incompatible with the extractor’s initial-frame
assumptions, derive a valid initialization explicitly. Do not silently apply
the round-initial-cut assumption to unsuitable data.

3. Test the unchanged A evolution on nonradiative data.

Run the complete native evolution → recorder → extraction pipeline for:
 a. A stationary Schwarzschild control.
 b. A time-dependent gauge perturbation of the same physical geometry.

Use valid vacuum spatial geometry and extrinsic curvature. Construct the
initial lapse/shift perturbation and auxiliary gauge variables consistently.
Prefer a compact perturbation initially away from scri, including angular
dependence so that the angular frame treatment is exercised.

Do not mistake a perturbation of physical constraint data for a pure gauge
perturbation. Verify the initial constraints and boundary compatibility.

Determine which Moxon coordinate cases, if any, can be represented by exact
solutions of our unchanged gauge. For the others, use compatible native gauge
dynamics as counterparts and state clearly that they are not exact
reproductions of the paper’s prescribed coordinates.

Run long enough for the gauge disturbance to affect the outer region and for
post-disturbance behavior to be measured. Determine the duration from the
actual characteristic propagation and observed evolution, rather than choosing
an arbitrary short endpoint.

4. Record the minimum diagnostics needed to explain any failure.

At matching points/times, before reducing extrema, record:
- Lapse-equation compatibility and its signed derivative contributions.
- Nullness of scri.
- The full four-dimensional tensor
    T_ab = nabla_a nabla_b Omega - g_ab Box(Omega)/4.
- Cut-metric defects and Bondi-time/angular transport errors.
- News across all retained modes.
- Relevant pole/h, penalty and normal-lift derivative diagnostics already
  available in the code.

Compute T_ab independently from:
 A. Native spatial derivatives and the actual endpoint evolution rates.
 B. Native spatial derivatives and time differences of accepted records.

Account for algebraic projections and fixed endpoint lapse/shift when comparing
these paths. Do not compare a pre-projection stage RHS directly with an accepted
projected-state derivative without deriving the relationship.

Check the lapse fixed-point identity from the implemented equations, including
its assumptions and finite-resolution correction terms. Alpha=1 after
projection is not itself a test of lapse-equation compatibility.

For nonradiative exact analytic data, verify that the tensor and radiation
diagnostics have the expected zero limits. A small tangential shear alone is
not sufficient.

5. Establish numerical convergence economically.

Use three resolutions for the principal native dynamical comparison.
Refine the actual boundary derivative support, not only the harmonic cutoff.

Separate:
- Spatial error.
- Evolution timestep error.
- Recorder cadence/time-differentiation error.
- Angular frame interpolation error.

Include a half-timestep control for the representative gauge-dynamical case.
Reuse dense records for cadence comparisons where possible.

Compare consistent physical cuts and polarization conventions. Account for
roundoff floors and the expected order of differentiated quantities.

Report both absolute false-news errors and their convergence. Do not choose
an arbitrary tolerance merely because a run passes it. Explain how the
measured error floor would limit subsequent weak-wave extraction.

6. Diagnose rather than conceal discrepancies.

For every failure, identify whether it first appears in:
- Analytic coordinate/conformal conversion.
- Exact-derivative geometry or frame extraction.
- Native spatial derivative reconstruction.
- Recorded-time differentiation.
- A’s evolved boundary geometry.
- Angular seams, normal lifts or projection consistency.

Do not remove conformal corrections, subtract a fitted waveform background,
force cuts to appear round, or change A to obtain zero news.

A genuinely isolated implementation bug may be fixed with a minimal regression
test and a before/after comparison. Changes to A’s mathematical treatment must
remain separate proposals, not part of the baseline validation.
