\begin{abstract}
The new scheme replaces 55 independently stored conformal fields with 50 intrinsic fields and gives all ten gradient families a common transport--relaxation subsidiary equation. This removes identifiable continuum reduction-source mixing and enforces conformal algebraic constraints by parametrization. It does not yet establish stable puncture evolution. A coarse uniform single-hole run developed large physical Hamiltonian error and stopped when $\alpha$ exceeded 2 near $11.03M$. Finer and SMR screens were incomplete when the investigation was stopped.

The earlier projected scheme evolved a head-on binary through a merger feature, developed sustained constraint growth around $40M$, and failed at total coordinate time $73.79991M$ with a non-positive conformal metric. Its global GH and auxiliary resets, different grid, damping, gauge switch, and off-reduction equations make this an informative historical comparison, not a matched performance experiment. Its early projection control was corrective; a causal explanation of its late failure remains incomplete.

A plausible synthesis retains the intrinsic geometry and complete derivative equations while treating any localized gradient reconstruction as an explicit numerical closure. That is a proposal for review, not an implemented or tested improvement. No evolution, build, or scientific test was run to prepare this document.
\end{abstract}

\section{Common conventions and the state dictionary}
Use $G=c=1$, signature $(-,+,+,+)$, and
\begin{equation}
\D=\partial_t-\beta^i\partial_i,\quad \alpha=\rho w,\quad
\gamma_{ij}=w^{-2}g_{ij},\quad
K^{\rm phys}_{ij}=w^{-2}(A_{ij}+g_{ij}K/3).
\end{equation}
$K^{\rm phys}_{ij}=-\tfrac12\mathcal L_n\gamma_{ij}$. All conformal contractions below use $g$ and $g^{-1}$. Configuration fields are dimensionless; $K,A,C,Z$ and first derivatives have units $M^{-1}$. Denote the spacetime metric by $\psi_{\mu\nu}$ to avoid confusing it with $g_{ij}$.

On the full reduction surface, the covariant reference is
\begin{equation}
R_{\mu\nu}-\nabla_{(\mu}C_{\nu)}+
\kappa\left[n_{(\mu}C_{\nu)}-\tfrac12\psi_{\mu\nu}n^\sigma C_\sigma\right]=0,
\qquad C_\mu=H_\mu+\Gamma_\mu.
\end{equation}
Here $C=n^\mu C_\mu=C_\perp$ and $Z^i=g^{ij}C_j$; the spatial coordinate projection of the raised spacetime constraint is $C^i+\beta^iC^0=w^2 Z^i$. This normalization is not an arbitrary Z4 convention. Both schemes use this physical identification on consistent jets. Their off-reduction extensions and numerical projections differ.

\subsection{Intrinsic algebraic geometry}
For $s=(a,c,b,d,e)$, let
\begin{equation}
T=\begin{pmatrix}e^a&0&0\\b&e^c&0\\d&e&e^{-a-c}\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 g=TT^T,\qquad A=T\widehat A T^T,\quad
 \widehat A_{33}=-\widehat A_{11}-\widehat A_{22}.
\end{equation}
The stored curvature entries are $(\widehat A_{11},\widehat A_{12},\widehat A_{13},\widehat A_{22},\widehat A_{23})$. Defining $J_{ij,A}=\partial g_{ij}/\partial s^A$ gives
\begin{equation}
\det g=1,\quad \tr(g^{-1}A)=0,\quad \tr(g^{-1}Q_k)=0,\quad Q_{kij}=J_{ij,A}S_k^A.
\end{equation}
These are exact algebraic identities in exact arithmetic, even when $S\ne\partial s$. They do not imply good conditioning, small physical constraints, or small curls. Floating-point exponent overflow or cancellation can still invalidate reconstructed geometry.

True composite derivatives are
\begin{align}
\partial_k g_{ij}&=J_{ij,A}\partial_k s^A,\\
\partial_k Q_{lij}&=J_{ij,A}\partial_k S_l^A+J_{ij,AB}(\partial_k s^B)S_l^A,\\
\partial_k g^{-1}&=-g^{-1}(\partial_k g)g^{-1},\\
\partial_k A&=(\partial_kT)\widehat AT^T+T(\partial_k\widehat A)T^T+T\widehat A(\partial_kT)^T.
\end{align}
The new kernel differentiates these functions using the actual primary jets. Replacing $\partial g$ by independent $Q$, or $\partial\alpha$ by independent $l$, would change its defining PDE.

The alpha-weighted momentum multiplies this last expression by $\alpha=\rho w$, yielding a regular last term $-3\rho A^j{}_i\partial_jw$. \subsection{All thirty auxiliary equations and the exact subsidiary law}
Define ten potentials, ten source functions, and their three gradients:
\begin{equation}
x^A=(w,\alpha,s^{1\ldots5},\beta^{1\ldots3}),\quad
G_i^A=(p_i,l_i,S_i^{1\ldots5},B_i{}^{1\ldots3}),\quad
F^A=(F_w,-2\alpha K,F_s^{1\ldots5},F_\beta^{1\ldots3}).
\end{equation}
The complete auxiliary rule is
\begin{equation}\boxed{
\partial_tG_i^A=\beta^j\partial_jG_i^A+(\partial_i\beta^j)G_j^A
+\partial_iF^A-\lambda(G_i^A-\partial_ix^A).}
\end{equation}
Every derivative of $F$ is a complete derivative of the composite source, holding no evolving field artificially constant. For example,
\begin{equation}
\partial_tl_i=\beta^j\partial_jl_i+(\partial_i\beta^j)l_j
-2K\partial_i\alpha-2\alpha\partial_iK-\lambda(l_i-\partial_i\alpha).
\end{equation}
Set $E_i^A=G_i^A-\partial_ix^A$ and $\Omega_{ij}^A=\partial_iG_j^A-\partial_jG_i^A$. Commuting smooth derivatives and subtracting the differentiated primary equation gives
\begin{align}
(\partial_t-\mathcal L_\beta)E^A&=-\lambda E^A,\\
(\partial_t-\mathcal L_\beta)\Omega^A&=-\lambda\Omega^A-d\lambda\wedge E^A.
\end{align}
Here $\mathcal L_\beta$ acts on the spatial covector index (and on both indices for the two-form); the family label is fixed. This removes the extra source map of the intermediate extension. It does not remove shift stretching or the variable-rate curl source. In the puncture screens, $\lambda=\alpha/M$; $\eta=2/M$ and $\kappa=1/M$. Thus relaxation becomes weak as the lapse collapses, and $d\lambda\wedge E$ can generate curl where the rate varies.

For fixed coordinate-volume norms and suitable boundary data, the smooth subsidiary equation yields, family by family,
\begin{equation}
\frac{d}{dt}\int |E|^2d^3x=
\int_{\partial\mathcal V}(\beta\cdot n)|E|^2dS+
2\int E^T\left[\operatorname{Sym}(\partial\beta)
-(\lambda+\tfrac12\partial_j\beta^j)I\right]E\,d^3x.
\end{equation}
Positive $\lambda$ alone therefore does not prove monotone coordinate-norm decay. Derivatives of the coefficients enter higher-order estimates. Nor does an exact continuum identity eliminate discrete forcing.

The physical metric curl remains
\begin{equation}
\partial_iQ_{jab}-\partial_jQ_{iab}
=J_{ab,A}\Omega_{ij}^A+
J_{ab,AB}\big[(\partial_is^B)S_j^A-(\partial_js^B)S_i^A\big].
\end{equation}
It vanishes for exact gradients, but is not simply $J\Omega$ off reduction. Intrinsic curls and the old raw $Q$ curl must not be compared as the same scalar diagnostic.

\subsection{Stored-gradient advection changes the off-reduction gauge}

Consequently the old composite lapse obeys

\subsection{Curvature/GH: equal on reduction, different extensions}
To recover the legacy curvature/GH rows from Section 3, store $g,A,Q$ directly and put $l=L/2$. Retain the displayed $K,C,F_A,Z$ structure with the following precise replacements:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Differentiate inverse metrics in $\partial\Lambda$ and $\partial(g^{-1}A)$ using $\widehat\partial g^{-1}=-g^{-1}Qg^{-1}$ rather than the true primary metric derivative. Differentiate the independent $Q$ directly, instead of differentiating $J(s)S$.
\item Use $\alpha wP_{ij}-w^2N_{ij}$ in $\mathcal S$, not the symmetrized Hessians. The old code stores the upper-triangle curvature RHS and mirrors the tensor representation. When curls are nonzero, this upper-triangle prescription is not the new symmetric tensor extension.
\item Omit $g^{ij}\mathcal B_j$ from $\D Z^i$.
\item Use $\kappa=0$ for the recorded collision, instead of the new screen's $\kappa=1/M$.
\end{enumerate}
There are no additional $K$ or $C$ algebraic source changes beyond these operator/normalization choices. Both have the GH contribution $\alpha(H^*-KC)$ in $K$ and $C$, and the same regular $\mathcal T$ expression. The chart evolution of $\widehat A$ is the tangent map of the new $F_A$, not an extra physical force.

The derivative difference already changes off-reduction curvature sources before projection:
\begin{equation}
\widehat\partial_i g^{-1}-\partial_i g^{-1}=-g^{-1}q_i g^{-1}.
\end{equation}
On smooth full-reduction jets, this vanishes, Hessian symmetrization changes nothing, and the $B$ curl vanishes. After matching the gauge function and damping, the physical equations can agree there. Such on-manifold agreement does not make the two algorithms equivalent after truncation and transfer errors are introduced.

\subsection{Projection is part of the old algorithm}
After each RK-stage update the legacy task chain restricts/exchanges, fills physical ghosts, prolongates, then enforces algebraic constraints and resets $C,Z$. On the final RK stage only, it reconstructs auxiliary gradients and performs an additional restriction/exchange/physical-BC/prolongation pass. The actual reset is
\begin{equation}
p_i^+=D_i^hw,\quad L_i^+=2(wD_i^h\rho+\rho D_i^hw),\quad
Q_{iab}^+=D_i^hg_{ab},\quad B_i{}^{a+}=D_i^h\beta^a.
\end{equation}
The factorized lapse target is not $2D_i^h(\rho w)$ at finite spacing. After the exchange, transferred ghosts need not equal the derivative of transferred primaries. This is why setting an active-cell reduction residual to zero does not establish globally compatible derivatives.

Writing $d_g=\det g$ and $s_g=d_g^{-1/3}$, the algebraic map is
\begin{align}
g^+&=s_g g,\\
A^+&=A-\tfrac13g\tr(g^{-1}A),\\
Q_i^+&=s_g[Q_i-\tfrac13g\tr(g^{-1}Q_i)],\\
C^+&=0,\qquad Z^{i+}=0\quad\hbox{(separate GH reset).}
\end{align}
In particular the legacy code does \emph{not} multiply $A^+$ by $s_g$. Strict metric validation precedes the algebraic map, so it cannot repair a non-SPD RK update. The new chart structurally removes these algebraic projection steps; intrinsic production rejects enabling either GH or auxiliary projection.

For the old projected algorithm, a zero post-reset $C$ or $Z$ is a construction, not evidence of satisfying the physical Einstein constraints. Projection does not directly set $H_{\rm phys}$ or $M_{\rm phys}$ to zero. It also means the free GH subsystem's spectrum alone cannot characterize the effective step map. Both projections and transfers belong in any analysis of that map.

\section{The implemented discretization is not the exact subsidiary PDE}
\subsection{A material lapse-target limitation in the new mesh kernel}
Source inspection at the pause is explicit: \src{intrinsic_finite_difference.hpp} computes $dU=D_hU$, calls \src{PointRHS}, and adds componentwise KO. Inside \src{intrinsic_rhs.hpp}, the lapse reduction target is
\begin{equation}
R^\alpha_{\rm code}=\rho D_hw+wD_h\rho,
\end{equation}
formed from the primary jets. It is \emph{not} the shared composite-stencil $D_h(\rho w)$ used in the separate direct-lapse helper/transfer-target work. The complete-source differentiation is analytic chain-rule differentiation evaluated on FD jets, not $D_h$ applied to a materialized complete source.

This is consistent with the written continuum equations but leaves a discrete product-rule defect:
\begin{equation}
\delta_h=\rho D_hw+wD_h\rho-D_h(\rho w),\quad
l-R^\alpha_{\rm code}=[l-D_h(\rho w)]-\delta_h.
\end{equation}
Relative to damping toward $D_h(\rho w)$, the implemented relaxation contains the extra forcing $+\lambda\delta_h$. Accordingly, the new production must not be described as having eliminated the direct composite lapse-target issue merely because another helper in the repository did so. This inspection is a documentation finding; no source change or new test was made. The already-failed direct-lapse stress comparison shows that fixing this alone is not known to cure puncture instability.

\subsection{Finite differences, RK, KO, and boundaries}
The screens use centered FD6, RK3, CFL .2, KO .3 on each stored field, and ordinary transfer. On a uniform mesh the first derivative is
\begin{equation}
D_hf_i=\frac{-f_{i-3}+9f_{i-2}-45f_{i-1}+45f_{i+1}-9f_{i+2}+f_{i+3}}{60h}.
\end{equation}
The FD6 KO stencil has order-eight differences and an $O(h^7)$ smooth modified-equation contribution. Applying KO to chart variables and $\widehat A$ differs from applying it directly to $g,A$ after a nonlinear map. Matching the numerical value .3 therefore does not match the physical dissipation operator across representations.

For a reconstruction $\mathcal R_h(U)$, the relevant discrete defect is
\begin{equation}
I_h=\dot G-\mathcal R_h'(U)\dot U-\mathcal T_h(G-\mathcal R_h(U))
+\lambda(G-\mathcal R_h(U)).
\end{equation}
It can be forced by product-rule errors, source differentiation, transfer, KO, physical boundaries, and RK composition. In particular,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal R_h^\alpha=D_h(\rho w),\qquad
(\mathcal R_h^\alpha)'\dot U=D_h(\rho\dot w+w\dot\rho).
\end{equation}
Nonlinear reconstruction is not exactly preserved by an ordinary RK update even if its continuous-time tangent relation is enforced. The scalar SSPRK3 stability polynomial $1+z+z^2/2+z^3/6$ is not by itself a coupled nonnormal/SMR stability proof.

Outflow uses componentwise polynomial ghost extrapolation (the selected order 2 is linear extrapolation) and a second-order radial Sommerfeld boundary RHS, schematically
\begin{equation}
\partial_tU\big|_{\rm face}=-\frac{x^i}{r}\partial_i^{(2)}U-\frac{U-U_\infty}{r}.
\end{equation}
The new flat state uses $w=\rho=1$, zero chart and zero remaining fields. These are not constraint-preserving GH boundary conditions. Gauge/constraint speeds need not equal the unit radial speed. The small $\pm8M$ domain permits early comparisons but cannot be treated as isolated at late times simply because the first bad cell is near the core.

\subsection{Legacy: a plausible discrete feedback loop, not a demonstrated cause}
\textbf{Measured:} first-order error growth near persistent interfaces precedes metric loss; active reconstruction was useful in an early control. \textbf{Conjecture:} transfer injects gradient inconsistency, curvature/gauge sources respond, and repeated projection/transfer reshapes the error into a growing mode. The observation that projection can remove a local reduction residual yet reintroduce derivative inconsistency through ghosts makes this plausible. It does not show projection is net destabilizing over the late trajectory.

An equally relevant alternative is that the evolved primary geometry/gauge already develops an unstable discrete mode, and increasingly large projection corrections are its symptom. Near loss of SPD, the inverse metric and algebraic normalization can magnify existing errors; that amplification alone cannot identify the earlier seed. The evidence does not warrant blaming the outer boundary or ruling it out for all characteristic sectors without a characteristic-domain analysis. The large outer domain makes the new $\pm8M$ boundary concern a materially different issue.

\subsection{Intrinsic: constraint/gauge growth with a different stopping surface}
\textbf{Measured:} the failed uniform grid has no refinement transfer interface. Thus the particular SMR commutator mechanism cannot be necessary for this failure. $H_{\rm phys}$ was already large before the stop, and $C,Z$ were no longer suppressed by resets. The exact subsidiary identity cannot prevent discrete forcing, free GH growth, or underresolved puncture derivatives.

\textbf{Conjecture:} on this coarse grid, underresolved puncture/gauge dynamics and the discrete gradient/source defects feed a coupled curvature/GH mode. Lapse-scaled reduction relaxation may become too weak during collapse to control accumulated errors. Negative $K$ subsequently raises the lapse through $\D\alpha=-2\alpha K$; the recorded negative $K$ is consistent with the final rising lapse. This sign observation does not prove the earlier causal sequence, nor does it prove that the $\alpha=2$ defect initiated the runaway. Physical deterioration precedes the threshold.

Further alternatives remain open: a boundary-driven gauge disturbance in the small domain, late timestep effects, nonnormal amplification, and representation-dependent KO/truncation near the puncture. The early small timestep difference does not exclude late-time amplification. The frozen CPU/CUDA point checks reduce the likelihood of simple bulk algebra/transcription errors in tested regimes; they do not exclude every mesh/RK/boundary issue. The incomplete SMR continuation is neither a stability pass nor a contrasting failure mechanism.

\subsection{Why the old method can survive longer without being a better continuum system}
The old reset imposes $E\approx0$ once per full step and $C=Z=0$ once per stage. The new method removes those numerical controls at the same time as changing the state, derivative completion, switch, and damping. Suppressing off-constraint modes can improve practical lifetime even when the unconstrained PDE is less transparent. Conversely, the new chart can prevent the old determinant sign failure in exact arithmetic while allowing another instability to become the first stopping event. Changing the terminal variable is not itself a cure.

The comparisons also change single hole to binary, $h$, core regularity, domain volume, topology, and diagnostic masks. Dividing $73.8$ by $11.03$ would be scientifically meaningless as a formulation speed or stability advantage. Neither current branch has the requested demonstrated physical field/constraint convergence through a healthy post-merger interval.

\section{Can the schemes combine their strengths?}
\subsection{A coherent candidate synthesis}
My best-supported proposal is to keep the new intrinsic configuration and curvature chart, true primary advection, complete source differentiation, and the common auxiliary law. These features address identifiable equation-level weaknesses without relying on repeated determinant/trace repairs. Preserve the legacy collision infrastructure (initial data, topology handling, tracking, extraction) as engineering infrastructure, after respecting the new state layout.

The useful legacy ingredient to reconsider is \emph{explicitly controlled auxiliary reconstruction}, rather than silently reinstating all global projections. A local closure could use synchronized same-stage primary ghosts and
\begin{equation}
G^+=G-P(G-\mathcal R_hx),\qquad
\mathcal R_hx=(D_hw,D_h(\rho w),D_hs,D_h\beta),\quad 0\le P\le1,
\end{equation}
while holding $w,\rho,s,\beta,K,\widehat A,C,Z$ fixed. Then $Q=J(s)S$ remains intrinsic. This borrows the old method's ability to constrain derivative drift, retaining the new algebraic geometry and free GH meaning. It was not implemented or tested in the intrinsic puncture campaign.

A full reconstruction $P=1$ before an RHS is a locally second-order-in-space numerical closure for those gradients, not the free first-order theorem. A taper has the continuum bookkeeping
\begin{equation}
E^+=(1-P)E,\qquad \Omega^+=(1-P)\Omega-dP\wedge E.
\end{equation}
Thus a taper can itself create curl. A moving or field-dependent mask adds stage/time dependence. A fixed fractional reset every stage corresponds formally to $\lambda_{\rm eff}\sim-\log(1-P)/\Delta t_{\rm event}$, not to a fixed physical damping rate as $\Delta t$ changes. These distinctions must remain explicit in any future design.

The first design decision is whether the intended method is the free intrinsic system or an intrinsic system with an explicitly delimited numerical core closure.
